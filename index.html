Aluminum Scrap: Types, Sorting and What Affects the Rate

Aluminum is one of the most valuable recyclable metals a factory throws away, and one of the easiest to undervalue. A lot of clean offcuts and a lot of mixed, coated and contaminated material can both be called "aluminum scrap", but they are priced very differently. Knowing the form of your scrap and how to keep it clean is the biggest thing you can do to get a fair offer.

Common forms of aluminum scrap

FormTypical sourcePoints to note
Sheet, plate and offcutsFabrication, cutting and stamping unitsEasy to assess. Keep free of steel and plastic fittings.
Extrusion and profile scrapWindow, frame and structure workAnodised or powder-coated pieces may be treated differently from bare metal.
Cast scrapMachine parts, housings, castingsCast and wrought aluminum are different alloy families. Keep them apart.
FoilPackaging, printing, pharma and food unitsThin material. Keep it clean and separate from laminated or paper-backed foil.
Alu-Alu and blister scrapPharmaceutical packagingA laminate of several layers, usually handled as its own category. Do not mix with solid scrap.
Turnings and boringsMachining and lathe workFine, often oily. Needs dry, clean storage.

What affects the rate

Form and gradeSolid, clean, single-alloy lots are generally valued above mixed or thin material.
CleanlinessOil, paint, plastic, rubber and steel inserts reduce the usable metal in a lot.
Metal contentLaminates and coated foil contain less recoverable aluminum per kilo than a solid offcut.
MoistureWet or oily scrap adds weight that is not metal, and buyers will usually adjust for it.
QuantityLarger, regular lots are easier to plan collection for and are usually preferred.
Market movementAluminum prices follow the wider metal market, so offers change over time.

How to sort and store your scrap

  • Separate by form. Keep sheet, castings, foil, blister scrap and turnings in separate bins or bags from the first day.
  • Remove non-aluminum parts. Take out steel screws, rubber, plastic handles and glass where practical.
  • Do a magnet check. Aluminum is not magnetic. If a magnet sticks, there is steel in the lot.
  • Keep it dry and off the floor. Use covered storage and avoid mixing oily turnings with clean material.
  • Count and weigh. Record approximate weights per category so quotes are based on real numbers.
Safety note. Aluminum edges are sharp, and very fine turnings or dust should be kept dry and away from heat and ignition sources. Use gloves when handling and follow your site's safety rules when storing fine material.

Preparing for a sale

Before you ask for a quote, collect a few clear photos of each category, an estimate of the quantity and the pickup location. Ask how the material will be weighed, whether your team can be present, and which documents you will receive with the pickup. If your plant produces aluminum scrap every month, agree a regular collection so the material never builds up.
