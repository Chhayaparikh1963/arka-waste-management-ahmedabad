E-Waste for Offices and Factories: What to Do With Old Equipment

Every office and factory ends up with a cupboard of dead electronics: old PCs, monitors, servers, printers, UPS units, routers and a tangle of cables. It feels like scrap, but it is not ordinary scrap. Electronic equipment contains metals and materials that are valuable to recover and harmful if handled badly, and the drives inside it often hold business or personal data. This guide sets out what to do before it leaves your premises.

What counts as e-waste in a workplace

IT equipment

Desktops, laptops, monitors, servers, switches, routers, printers.

Power equipment

UPS units, adaptors, chargers and their batteries.

Cables and peripherals

Power cords, network cables, keyboards, mice, scanners.

Security systems

CCTV cameras, DVRs, biometric and access-control devices.

Communication devices

Phones, intercoms, handsets and conferencing gear.

Plant electronics

Control panels, controllers, instruments and display units.

Before anything leaves the building

  1. Make a list. Record each item, its type and serial number or asset tag, and where it sits. This is the basis of your handover record.
  2. Get approval. Have the right person sign off on the disposal and update your asset register.
  3. Protect the data. Remove or securely erase drives, phones and any storage media, and keep a record that you did it. If a drive cannot be wiped, ask about physical destruction.
  4. Check for reuse. Equipment that still works may be better refurbished or reused than recycled.
  5. Gather it in one place. Store items together, dry and secure, with batteries kept apart.
Rules apply. Organisations that hold electronic equipment are generally expected to pass it only to authorised collection or recycling channels and keep records. Requirements change, so check the current position under the E-Waste (Management) Rules with the pollution control board or a compliance consultant.

Do and do not

Do

  • Keep equipment whole and intact
  • Wipe or remove storage media first
  • Hand over only to authorised recyclers
  • Get a handover record and certificate
  • Keep batteries separate

Do not

  • Sell to anyone who cannot show authorisation
  • Strip boards or break open monitors yourself
  • Burn cables to get at the copper
  • Mix e-waste into ordinary scrap
  • Leave devices in the rain or the open

Common questions

Can I sell old computers to a scrap dealer?

It is safer not to. Without authorisation, equipment may be stripped or burned informally, and you will not get the paperwork you need.

Do I have to remove the hard drives?

Data security is your responsibility. Either wipe the drives thoroughly or remove them, and keep a record of what you did.

What about UPS batteries?

Batteries are handled under their own rules. Keep them separate from other e-waste and ask the recycler how they will be managed.
